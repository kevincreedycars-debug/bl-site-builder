# Strategy

What the portfolio is for, in what order it gets built, and how a decision gets
made when two good things compete.

## The business, in one paragraph

A small number of WordPress sites, each on a subject narrow enough to be an
authority in it, sell links to buyers who need a citation from a real site. The
links pay. The content is what makes the sites worth linking from, and it is also
what makes them survive an update. The sending mailboxes exist so outreach,
delivery and replies do not depend on one site's host. The citation work makes
the business entity legible to a search engine and to a buyer who checks who they
are dealing with.

## The order, and why

1. **Sites reachable.** Nothing else can be measured until a site can be read.
2. **Content that can be approved.** A piece with no claim ledger cannot be
   approved, so research comes before drafting, every time.
3. **Links verified after placement.** A link is sold as live. "Placed" is a
   claim; "verified with a 200 and the anchor matching" is evidence.
4. **Mail on its own domains.** Reputation is per domain and per mailbox. Shared
   it is lost together.
5. **Citations from one set of details.** Name, address and phone, agreed once,
   copied everywhere. A wrong phone number is worse than no listing at all.

The order is not a preference. Each step is the input to the next, and skipping
one means the later step measures noise.

## What "good" looks like

- A buyer can look at any sold link and see when it was last checked and what
  came back.
- Every published sentence on a site is traceable to a source in the ledger, or
  is an opinion clearly written as one.
- The business details are the same on every listing, and there is one place
  they are edited.
- Nothing is sent from a domain that is not authenticated.
- A number on the dashboard is never older than a day without the page saying so.

## What is refused

- Publishing to a URL nobody confirmed.
- A draft approved without a ledger.
- Sending from a domain that fails authentication, because the cost of a bad
  send is paid by every later send.
- A link marked verified on the strength of the placement report alone.
- A second site in a subject that the first site has not yet earned authority in.

## How the two goals compete

Speed of publication against the ledger: the ledger wins. A piece that takes
three days to source and is right beats four pieces that are fast and unsourced,
because the second kind is what makes a site worthless in an update.

Volume of links against reputation: reputation wins. A sold link on a site that
returns 401 for a week costs one refund; the pattern costs the domain.
